# Why you should use obsidian note? 

> *“Your mind is for having ideas, not holding them.”* – David Allen

Over the years, I’ve tried many note-taking apps: Evernote, OneNote, Notion, Google Keep... But none of them gave me the level of **control**, **flexibility**, and **ownership** that I found with **Obsidian**.

---

## 🧠 Why I Chose Obsidian

When I started managing multiple technical projects, learning paths, and personal goals, I realized that:

- I needed something **offline** and **local**.
- I wanted to organize my notes like a **second brain**, not like a linear notebook.
- I didn’t want to rely on proprietary formats or platforms.

That’s when I discovered Obsidian.
  Obsidian stores everything in **plain Markdown files**. That means:

- Your notes are yours forever.
- They’re portable, version-controllable (with Git), and future-proof.

---
## 📓 A Quick Guide to Markdown

Markdown is the core of Obsidian — it’s a lightweight markup language that makes formatting fast and intuitive. Here are some basics:


| Feature           | Markdown Syntax Example               | Output                    |
|------------------|----------------------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Heading**       | `# Title` / `## Subtitle` / `### H3`  | Large, medium, small titles |
| **Bold**          | `**bold text**`                       | **bold text**             |
| *Italic*          | `*italic text*`                       | *italic text*             |
| ***Bold+Italic*** | `***bold italic***`                   | ***bold italic***         |
| `Code`            | `` `inline code` ``                   | `inline code`             |
| Code Block        | \`\`\`python<br>print("Hello")<br>\`\`\` (see below) | Code block            |
| Link              | `[text](https://example.com)`         | [my github](https://github.com/ahpatsum15) |
| Image             | `![alt text](path/to/image.jpg)`      | ![favicon](/assets/images/favicon.ico) |
| Bullet List       | `- Item 1`<br>`- Item 2`              | - Item 1<br>- Item 2      |
| Numbered List     | `1. First item`<br>`2. Second item`   | 1. First item<br>2. Second item |
| Checkbox          | `- [ ] Task`<br>`- [x] Done`          | - [ ] Task<br>- [x] Done  |
| Blockquote        | `> Quoted text`                       | > Quoted text             |

## 🔥 Favorite Features

Here are the features that made Obsidian an essential part of my daily workflow:

### 1. [[Wikilinks]] and Backlinks
You can easily link notes together using `[[note name]]`, and Obsidian automatically builds a **graph of connections**. It feels like building your own personal Wikipedia.

### 2. Graph View 🌐
A beautiful visual representation of how your notes are linked together. Great for seeing the big picture of your knowledge base.

### 3. Daily Notes + Templates 🗓️
I use Daily Notes to journal tasks, ideas, and thoughts. With templates, I can insert predefined structures — like my morning planning checklist or project logs — instantly.

### 4. Tags and Search 🔍
Tags help with flexible categorization. Advanced search makes it easy to find any snippet, even in thousands of notes.

### 5. Plugins Ecosystem 🧩
The community plugins are mind-blowing. Some of my must-haves:
- **Calendar**
- **Dataview** (query your notes like a database!)
- **Kanban** boards
- **Advanced Tables**
- **Periodic Notes**
- **Tasks Plugin**
- **iconize**

### 6. Customizable Hotkeys and Themes 🎨
From light to dark modes, minimal to cyberpunk vibes, you can shape the UI to your taste. I personally use a minimal theme that helps me focus.

### 7. Local-first + Sync Option
Even though Obsidian is local-first, they offer an optional sync service (paid).

## ✍️ How I Use Obsidian Daily

- **Studying & Research**: I take notes on books, courses, and papers — linking related concepts as I go.
- **Project Management**: Each project has its own folder, with meeting notes, todos, and plans.
- **Blog & Content Creation**: I draft blog posts (like this one!) in Obsidian before publishing.
- **Personal Knowledge Base**: Everything I learn — from cryptography to DevSecOps — lives in my vault.
